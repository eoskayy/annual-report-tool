# Reflection

The first idea I derived for my agent was for it to be an investment guide that people can ask about certain industries they want to invest in based on the companies' profits, with the agent analyzing the open databases for best picks. However, later I realized that there is obviously no "correct" answer. Similarly, the lists really vary since each uses different parameters, and I had conflicting results in my first attempts. Consequently, I decided to make it an agent that gathers the data from company annual reports and makes comparisons on given metrics.

To set up my agent, I chose three automotive companies, BMW Group, Mercedes-Benz, and Toyota, and developed and tested the workflow on their 2025 company publications. The first problem I faced was that the companies define profit differently: Mercedes-Benz includes equity-method results and some financial items in EBIT, while BMW reports them below its profit row. Thus, I kept each company's printed profit measure, named each margin after it, and required the agent to flag the difference instead of forcing a ranking. Toyota's integrated report contained only summary figures, so I added its audited Form 20-F and kept the integrated report as a test of whether the agent recognises a summary-only document. Before running anything, I recorded my expectation in plan.md: at least 32 of 36 fields correct, with errors most likely in column, year, and scope selection.

The frozen outputs of all three runs (supervised, autonomous, fresh session) scored 36 of 36 against the reference table I had filled in by hand beforehand. Part of the reason is the constraints and control mechanisms I put in. However, the score also overstates what the tool can do: reviewing the agent's proposals, I found that it planned to copy page numbers from its own earlier notes, and the row labels the tool reads were supplied by the agent rather than discovered. All four documents had also been inspected during development, so there was no unseen test.

On the other hand, two things went wrong before the first output was frozen. First, the script could not isolate the Mercedes-Benz revenue cell, because a column of note references sits between the row label and the values. Second, it found two candidate opening dates for Toyota, because the statement of changes in equity shows an opening balance for each year. In both cases, the tool stopped instead of guessing. I do not read this as the agent being careful by nature; it stopped because I had written "stop if the coded rules cannot decide" into the plan.

My own work was not error-free. My first instruction asked for "profits", which is ambiguous, and for an emissions metric outside the agreed scope, and I initially took Toyota's margin to be exactly 10 when the unrounded figure is 9.983. The layout of my reference workbook and the scoring sheet were prepared with help from an AI assistant; the values and verdicts are mine.

Next time I would rely on an agent for reading clearly labelled printed tables, provided that abstention is written into its rules, but not for judging whether differently named measures are equivalent: I confirmed that difference myself by comparing the income statements. The three runs agreed on every value, but they used the same model and plan, so they are not independent. I would also reserve one unseen report and test a second industry before claiming the tool generalises.
